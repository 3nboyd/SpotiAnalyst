Dataset and Setup

The dataset has popular Spotify tracks and information like stream count, tempo, danceability, energy, valence, release year, and musical mode. The original file has 953 rows. One value in streams does not cleanly convert into a number, so stream calculations use only valid stream rows.

Descriptive Statistics

I used sample standard deviation for the spread. The stream counts and playlist counts have really big spreads, which makes sense because a few songs are huge hits while many others are still popular but not at the same level. The median is also lower than the mean for streams, which is another clue that the data is pulled upward by a few very large values.

1. Frequency Table: Danceability

Most songs fall in the middle-to-high danceability ranges. So, in this sample, the most-streamed songs usually are not low-danceability songs. They tend to be songs people could at least somewhat dance to.

2. Frequency Polygon / Bar Graph

The bar graph and the frequency polygon show the same idea in two ways. Danceability is highest around the 70-79 group, then it drops off for the very highest danceability scores.

3. Pie Chart: Musical Mode

More songs are in Major mode than Minor mode. This does not mean minor songs are unpopular, but the biggest Spotify songs in this dataset lean more toward Major.

4. Histogram: Danceability

The histogram makes it easier to see that danceability is not spread out evenly. Most songs are around 50% to 80%, and there are not many songs at the very low end. Since the bars are mostly stacked toward the higher side, the distribution looks slightly left-skewed for danceability.

5. Cumulative Frequency Plot

The cumulative frequency starts increasing a lot after the 50% mark. That tells me a lot of the dataset is being collected in the middle and upper-middle danceability ranges. By the time it reaches the 70-79 interval, over half of the songs have already been counted.

6. Stem-and-Leaf Plot: BPM

The stem-and-leaf plot groups BPM by tens but still lets me see the actual values. Most of the tempos are around the normal pop-song range, especially from about 90 to 140 BPM.
7. Box Plot: Streams
The box plot is very right-skewed. A lot of songs have hundreds of millions of streams, but a smaller number have extremely high stream counts, so they show up as clear outliers. The long right side also shows why the mean stream count is higher than the median.
8. Scatter Plot: Danceability vs Energy
Conclusion
Overall, the Spotify hits in this dataset usually have moderately high danceability and energy. The stream counts are much more spread out than the audio-feature percentages, which shows that popularity is not evenly shared across songs. Some songs are massive outliers. The graphs also show categorical patterns, like Major vs Minor mode, and numerical patterns, like skewness, cumulative frequency, and the relationship between danceability and energy.
